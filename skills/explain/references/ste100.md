# ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English: working rules

ASD-STE100 is a controlled language. AECMA started it in 1979 for aircraft maintenance manuals, so that
a mechanic who reads English as a second language reads a procedure one way only. Today it is also used
in defence, rail, and other industries. The specification has two parts:

- **Part 1: Writing rules** in nine sections (words, noun clusters, verbs, sentences, procedures,
  descriptive writing, safety instructions, punctuation and word counts, writing practices).
- **Part 2: Dictionary** of about 900 approved words. Each approved word has one meaning and one part of speech.
  You can also use the technical names and technical verbs of your own field.

The official specification is a free download from asd-ste100.org. This file is a working summary,
written for this skill. It is not a copy of the specification.

---

## Strict mode (`ste`)

### Words
1. Use approved words, technical names, and technical verbs only.
2. Use a word only in its approved meaning and part of speech. "Close" is a verb ("Close the valve"). It is not
   an adjective meaning "near". "Test" is a verb. Do not use it as a noun in a procedure if a verb does the job.
3. One word for one thing. Use the same term every time. Do not change "pump" to "unit" to "device" for variety.
4. Keep the small words. Do not delete "the", "a", "this", or "that" to make text short.
5. Do not use phrasal verbs ("set up", "carry out", "fill in"). Use one verb ("install", "do", "write").
6. Do not use slang, idioms, metaphors, or contractions ("don't", "it's").

### Noun clusters
7. A noun cluster has three words maximum ("hydraulic pump pressure"). Break a longer cluster with a preposition:
   "pressure of the hydraulic pump in the left system".

### Verbs
8. Approved forms only:

   | Form                          | Example               | Status        |
   |-------------------------------|-----------------------|---------------|
   | Imperative (command)          | Close the valve.      | Approved      |
   | Simple present                | The valve closes.     | Approved      |
   | Simple past                   | The valve closed.     | Approved      |
   | Simple future                 | The valve will close. | Approved      |
   | Infinitive                    | Turn the knob to close it. | Approved |
   | Past participle as adjective  | The closed valve      | Approved      |
   | Progressive (-ing)            | The valve is closing. | Not approved  |
   | Perfect                       | The valve has closed. | Not approved  |
   | Passive in procedures         | The valve must be closed. | Not approved |

9. The -ing form is allowed only inside a technical name ("landing gear").
10. Use the active voice. In descriptive text the passive is allowed only when the actor is unknown or does not matter.

### Sentences
11. Procedural sentence: 20 words maximum. Descriptive sentence: 25 words maximum.
12. One instruction per sentence. Exception: two actions that you do at the same time
    ("Push and hold the button").
13. One topic per paragraph. A descriptive paragraph has six sentences maximum.

### Procedures
14. Write instructions as commands (imperative).
15. Put a condition before the instruction: "If the light comes on, stop the engine."
16. Use a vertical numbered list for steps. One step per item.
17. Put a warning or caution before the step it applies to, not after.

### Descriptive writing
18. Put the most important information first. Start the paragraph with its topic sentence.
19. Use connectors to show the logic: "because", "if", "then", "but", "thus".
20. Use a vertical list when a sentence holds a long series of items.

### Safety instructions
21. **WARNING** = risk of injury or death. **CAUTION** = risk of damage to equipment.
22. Start with a clear, simple command. Then give the risk in a second sentence:
    `WARNING: Do not touch the brake unit until it is cool. Hot parts can cause injury.`

### Punctuation
23. Do not use semicolons. Make two sentences.
24. Use a colon before a list.
25. Do not hide important information in parentheses.

### Writing practices
26. Be specific. "Tighten the bolt to 12 N·m", not "Tighten the bolt correctly".
27. Do not write "etc." Give the full list or say "for example".

## 80% mode (`ste80`, the default)

Karpathy's note: the full specification is quite stringent, so ask for something like "80% of the way".
In this mode keep the rules that make text easy to read, and relax the ones that cost meaning.

**Keep (hard rules):**
- Sentences of 20 words or fewer for steps, 25 or fewer for descriptions. A few longer ones are OK if splitting them hurts the logic.
- One idea per sentence. One topic per paragraph. Six sentences maximum per paragraph.
- Active voice by default.
- One term per concept, used every time. Define each technical term at first use, in one short sentence.
- Most important point first. Conclusion before detail.
- Conditions before instructions. Numbered lists for steps.
- No filler, no hedging chains ("it might be worth considering that"), no idioms, no hype words.
- Concrete numbers and names instead of "some", "various", "significantly".

**Relax:**
- Any common English word is allowed. Prefer the short, common word ("use", not "utilize").
- The -ing form and the passive are allowed when they read more naturally.
- Analogies and examples are allowed. Mark them as examples ("For example, ...").
- Headings, tables, and bold key terms are encouraged.

## Before / after

Original:
> It is imperative that the operator ensures the hydraulic reservoir is replenished prior to commencing operation.

STE (13 words, limit 20):
> Make sure that the hydraulic reservoir is full before you start the operation.

Original (descriptive):
> Fuel is being supplied to the engine by the pump whenever the switch has been turned on.

STE (12 words, limit 25):
> The pump supplies fuel to the engine when the switch is on.

## Self-check (do this before you hand over)

1. Count the words in each sentence. Split any sentence over the limit.
2. Scan for -ing verbs, "has/have/had + verb", and "is/are/was/were + -ed" forms.
3. Scan for two words that name the same thing. Pick one.
4. Compare with the source. Restore any fact you dropped.
